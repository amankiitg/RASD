NEW F4 (main text, quoted in full)
=================================================================
"At 128 k, each decode token requires 7 rotations over the 32 patched layers
(src/models/ring_attention_kernel.py:402 loops over the ranks and line 426 issues a rotation
only while step < world_size - 1), so 224 rounds of a batch_isend_irecv ring per token,
measured under the NCCL blocking wait of Section 3, in which PyTorch's WorkNCCL::wait polls
with a fixed 10 ms host sleep (kSynchronizeBusyWaitMillis, ProcessGroupNCCL.cpp:426 in the
pinned torch 2.5.1). Figure 4 measures that primitive with no model attached, over five
payloads from 4 KB to 266 MB, in both wait modes in one session on one box.

Under the blocking wait every payload costs the same ~10.3 ms: 10.3347 ms for 4 KB against
10.3520 ms for 266 MB, an 0.4% spread across a 66,584x payload range. That flat line is the
poll, not the wire. Without the flag the same primitive takes 0.134 ms at 4 KB and rises
with payload to 2.619 ms at 266 MB, which is 101.7 GB/s, and the two modes' post-sync times
agree to within 3% at every payload, so the wire never changed.

... Table 2 is the headline, with the same cells plotted in Appendix C. Decode-only, the fix
is worth 2.80x / 3.77x / 4.38x with speculation and 3.30x / 4.26x / 5.32x without it at
128 k / 512 k / 1 M, and the mechanism is in what it removes rather than in the ratio.
Dividing the removed time by the 224 rounds: at 128 k it is 2.43 s per token, or 10.9 ms per
round, which is the poll period itself, and the microbench predicted 224 x (10.3347 - 2.6191)
ms = 1.72 s against a measured 1.75 s, within 1.4%. At 512 k and 1 M the removed time is far
larger, 7.64 and 13.88 s per token, because the key/value shard a round ships grows by 4x and
8x: the transfer no longer fits inside one poll period, and under blocking wait the host
cannot enqueue round i+1 until round i completes, so the next transfer cannot overlap the
current compute. The rotation's cost scales with the data it ships, and the flat ~10 ms per
round is a 128 k-payload measurement."
