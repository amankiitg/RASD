\title{What Limits Speculative Decoding at a Million Tokens?\\
       Greedy Loops, Rotary Stretching, and the Ring's Decode Step}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Speculative decoding's short-context speedups do not survive million-token inference, and the
reason is not the mechanism the technique's own literature would predict. We report three
scientific findings and two systems measurements on 8$\times$A100 80\,GB SXM4 with FP4 weights
and an NF4 KV cache. \emph{First}, the acceptance such campaigns report is measured on
degenerate text: under greedy decoding 9 of 10 rows in the released long-context first-window
audit loop, and 1 of 5 rows in the 128\,k greedy arm does, against 1 of 77 sampled rows at
$T{=}0.7$. A greedy draft accepts a loop trivially, so a greedy acceptance number measures the
text. \emph{Second}, stretching the rotary basis of a model that ships the scaling dictionary
costs \textbf{27 to 31\%} of speculative acceptance over five books while a paired perplexity
check on one passage, whose native perplexity is 1.013, moves by $+0.05\%$ and $+1.56\%$.
Acceptance and perplexity dissociate, and on this model the perplexity check did not reveal
the acceptance loss. \emph{Third}, a natively 1M-trained target stays coherent at 1\,M, within
$9.5\%$ of its own 128\,k perplexity on the exactly aligned book. \emph{Fourth}, our ring's
decode bottleneck was not the ring but a host-side poll: 224 rounds of a
\texttt{batch\_isend\_irecv} ring per token, each of which cost the same ${\sim}10.3$\,ms under
the blocking NCCL wait every run in this paper used, whatever the payload. Removing the
rotation with decode-side context parallelism, which is prior work, is worth $2.80$ to
$4.38\times$ decode-only with speculation and $3.30$ to $5.32\times$ without. \emph{Fifth},
speculation still pays $1.54$ to $2.29\times$ on top, and the matched 1\,M acceptance is
unchanged ($-0.1\%$). Against vLLM at bf16 with greedy decoding, which is not a
precision-matched comparison, vLLM's entire run takes $2.04$ to $2.57\times$ our prefill while
its decode stays $130$ to $162\times$ faster per token than ours.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Serving a million-token context on eight commodity GPUs forces two problems at once. The KV
cache does not fit, which ring attention \citep{liu2023ringattention} solves by sharding the
sequence dimension and rotating key/value blocks around the ranks. And every generated token
still costs a full forward pass, which speculative decoding
\citep{leviathan2023fast,chen2023accelerating} attacks by having a small draft propose several
tokens and verifying them with one target forward. We built \textbf{RASD}, a ring-attention
engine with an NF4 KV cache, an attention-sink prefix, rotary scaling where a target needs it,
and speculative verification, and asked whether speculation still pays at 1\,M. The
measurements kept saying that something else was wrong, so the findings below lead with the
science and close on our own engine.

\paragraph{F1. Acceptance and perplexity dissociate under rotary stretching.}
On \texttt{meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B}, a model that ships the \texttt{rope\_scaling} dictionary
the intervention edits, stretching the rotary basis at 128\,k costs \textbf{27 to 31\%} of
speculative acceptance over five books, from $0.7272$ to $0.5336$ (factor 16) and $0.5006$
(factor 32), while a paired perplexity check on a single passage moves by $+0.05\%$ and
$+1.56\%$. The arms differ in nothing but \texttt{rope\_factor}. Position scaling is judged by
perplexity; on this model that measure did not reveal the acceptance loss.
% SRC: scripts/lcfm_figures.py rope_arms(); results/mlsys/sampling_session/session_a_ROPE_SAMP_{NATIVE,F16,F32}_SPEC.csv; session_a_rope_fix_gate.csv

\paragraph{F2. Greedy acceptance numbers at long context are measured on degenerate text.}
Under greedy decoding, 9 of 10 rows in the released long-context first-window audit degenerate
into repetition, and 1 of 5 rows in the 128\,k greedy arm does, against 1 of 77 rows sampled
at $T{=}0.7$; that one row is a rotary variant and \S\ref{sec:res-degeneracy} names it. Rows
are classified by their recorded temperature, not by their stage name. A greedy acceptance
number therefore measures the degeneracy and not the agreement between draft and target, which
is why this campaign moved to sampling and why every row here carries a periodicity verdict.
% SRC: scripts/lcfm_figures.py census(); results/mlsys/natural_f1_128k_first_window.csv; repetition_SA_TEMP_0_128K_SPEC.csv

\paragraph{F3. A natively long-context target is coherent at 1\,M.}
On the one exactly aligned book, the target's own perplexity at 1\,M is $1.805$ against
$1.649$ at 128\,k, a $+9.5\%$ move; on the two books that share 91 to 95\% of their scored
tokens it is within $1.1\%$. Long context is not intrinsically hostile to speculation, which
is what made the two systems findings below worth chasing.
% SRC: scripts/lcfm_figures.py; results/mlsys/sampling_session/QUALITY_FIX_{128K,1M}_*.csv; alignment.csv

\paragraph{F4. Our ring's decode step was a host-side poll, not the ring.}
At 128\,k each decode token needs 7 rotations over the 32 patched layers, so 224 rounds of a
\texttt{batch\_isend\_irecv} ring
(\path{src/models/ring_attention_kernel.py:402} issues a rotation per
\texttt{step < world\_size - 1}). Every run in this paper measured that ring with PyTorch's
NCCL blocking wait (\texttt{TORCH\_NCCL\_BLOCKING\_WAIT=1}), in which each \texttt{wait()}
polls its collective with a fixed 10\,ms host sleep. A bare-process benchmark of the primitive,
with no model attached, costs the same ${\sim}10.3$\,ms per round at 4\,KB and at 266\,MB under
that flag, and 0.134\,ms rising to 2.619\,ms (101.7\,GB/s) without it: the flat line was the
poll, and the wire is bandwidth-bound above a few hundred kilobytes. Removing the rotation with
decode-side context parallelism, which is prior work \citep{yang2024contextparallelism}, is
worth \textbf{2.80$\times$ / 3.77$\times$ / 4.38$\times$} decode-only with speculation and
\textbf{3.30$\times$ / 4.26$\times$ / 5.32$\times$} without it at 128\,k / 512\,k / 1\,M on
matched cells, and the time it removes grows with context, 2.43 to 13.88\,s per token, because
the shard a round ships grows by 8$\times$ over that range. The flag could not simply be
dropped: without it the engine emits a different token stream in the one speculation cell
measured in both modes and fails an assertion in three of five stages, so split-KV without the
flag was not measured.
% SRC: scripts/lcfm_figures.py bench_two_modes(), removed_time(), headline_rows(); results/mlsys/bw_diag_p1/bench/, results/mlsys/bw_diag_p2/

\paragraph{F5. Speculation still pays, and the 1\,M acceptance is unchanged.}
With the rotation gone, speculation is worth $1.54\times$ / $2.29\times$ / $2.20\times$
decode-only over the same engine's target-only path at 128\,k / 512\,k / 1\,M, measured on
matched books only: one book at 128\,k, three at 512\,k, five at 1\,M. Acceptance at 1\,M
under the fixed engine shows no detectable net change against the corrected old-engine
baseline, $-0.1\%$ over 13 matched cells with a 95\% interval of about $\pm12\%$ relative
(per-cell changes run from $-31\%$ to $+30\%$), and the fixed kernel's target-only stream is bit-identical to the ring's for twelve
tokens before diverging where the baseline's own top-2 margin is 0.0000.
% SRC: scripts/lcfm_figures.py f5_rows(); results/mlsys/rerun_r1,r2,rerun_topup

\paragraph{What this paper does not claim.} The rotation-free decode kernel is prior work,
cited for the technique and never claimed: Meta's context-parallel report
\citep{yang2024contextparallelism} introduces exact ring variants for million-token inference,
including a pass-Q form that still circulates data around the ring each step, and vLLM's
Decode Context Parallelism \citep{lee2026dcp} is the production implementation of the
rotation-free merge used here. Our contribution is the diagnosis and the measurement. Every
timing in this paper was taken under the blocking NCCL wait of \S\ref{sec:setup}, and the
comparison against vLLM is \textbf{not precision-matched} (\S\ref{sec:res-vllm}).

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Long-context attention.} Ring attention \citep{liu2023ringattention} shards the
sequence across ranks and pipelines exact attention through a ring of exchanges, so per-rank
memory is independent of sequence length. The implementation here uses FlashAttention-2
\citep{dao2024flashattention2} as the local kernel. Ring Attention's own evaluation is about
training and long-context capability; what a decode step costs once every layer's key/value
shard must visit every rank is what \S\ref{sec:res-decode} measures.

\paragraph{Decode-side context parallelism (prior work).} The decode-phase gap is closed in
the literature, and we cite rather than claim it. Meta's context-parallel report
\citep{yang2024contextparallelism} introduces exact ring variants for million-token inference
(pass-KV and pass-Q); in pass-Q the query travels around the ring to meet the shard it needs,
followed by an all-to-all of partial outputs, so data still circulates once per step. What we
adopt, and what vLLM ships as Decode Context Parallelism \citep{lee2026dcp}, is the
rotation-free merge: the query is replicated, each rank attends to the shard it holds, and the
partials are combined by log-sum-exp. We call that \emph{split-KV}; the ring's per-step cost
is what \S\ref{sec:res-decode} is about.

\paragraph{Speculative decoding.} \citet{leviathan2023fast} and \citet{chen2023accelerating}
established draft-and-verify: a small model proposes $\gamma$ tokens, the target verifies them
in one pass, and the longest accepted prefix is kept with a residual resample for the rejected
position. Acceptance $\alpha$ is the fraction the target accepts, and $(\alpha^{\gamma+1} -
1)/(\alpha - 1)$ is the expected number of tokens the target verifies per forward pass, not a
wall-clock speedup; \citet{cai2024medusa} removed the separate draft model with multi-head
prediction. All of it is evaluated at short context, and the interaction with a distributed
KV cache is the setting here.

\paragraph{KV compression and position scaling.} NF4 quantization
\citep{dettmers2023qlora} shrinks the cache and an attention-sink prefix
\citep{xiao2024streamingllm} stabilises it. Rotary embeddings \citep{su2024rope} are stretched
past a model's training window by YaRN \citep{peng2023yarn}, an intervention that is
evaluated by perplexity among other measures, and usually validated by a perplexity curve
alone in practice; \S\ref{sec:res-rope} shows what that misses. The intervention here is not
YaRN: it changes the shipped \texttt{llama3} scaling factor of a model that already has one.

\section{System and Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{RASD in brief.} RASD runs on one node of 8$\times$A100 80\,GB SXM4, with the
target and a 1B draft (\texttt{meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B}) on every rank. Its KV cache is NF4
with chunked updates and a bf16 attention-sink prefix of the first 128 tokens; attention is
ring attention with FlashAttention-2 in each local block; the weights are \textbf{FP4}, since
NF4 is the KV codec. At 1\,M this campaign's rows record \textbf{29.6\,GiB allocated and
32.8\,GiB reserved on the worst rank} (35.6\,GiB reserved on the spec row), while the vanilla
HuggingFace stack runs out of memory at 128\,k single-rank. The long-context target needs no rotary
scaling at all (\texttt{rope\_type: none}); where an arm does need it, the engine applies a
fixed factor 8 at 8\,k and above, anchored on each context's own length, because the
\texttt{transformers} version in use ignores \texttt{original\_max\_position\_embeddings}.
% SRC: scripts/lcfm_figures.py memory_peak(); results/mlsys/bw_diag_p2/onem/blocking/df_arms.csv; results/mlsys/LCFM_RECONCILE.md (AUDIT E7/E8/E19)

\paragraph{Targets.} The long-context campaign runs the Gradient-1048k build of
\texttt{meta-llama}'s Llama-3-8B (\texttt{gradientai}, "Gradient-1048k"), trained to a
1\,M window, so nothing is stretched; the rotary intervention of \S\ref{sec:res-rope} runs
\texttt{meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B}, which ships the scaling dictionary under test. A ceiling test
on \texttt{meta-llama/Llama-2-7b-hf} is a capability reference only and no ratio here uses it
(Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}).
% SRC: results/lcfm/CAMERA_READY_KIT.md section 3e (limitation 4)

\paragraph{Protocol.} Prompts are PG-19 narrative \citep{rae2019pg19}, five books, windowed
so each rung's scored continuation ends at the same document position as the 1\,M rung, with
the alignment verified from \texttt{continuation\_sha256} and the recorded offsets rather than
from a pool name. Decoding is $T{=}0.7$ sampling, 128 new tokens, $\gamma{=}4$ speculative
steps, draft window cap 4096, seed 42 unless stated, and every row carries a repetition verdict
from the detector of \S\ref{sec:res-degeneracy}. Decode-only means
$(t_{\text{row}} - t_{\text{ttft}}) / (\text{tokens}-1)$. Every fixed-engine row records the
engine that produced it (\texttt{ring\_decode\_mode}, patched-layer count, git commit), so a
row that cannot prove its engine is void.
% SRC: scripts/lcfm_figures.py; results/mlsys/rerun_r1,r2 (provenance columns)

\paragraph{Every timing here was taken under NCCL blocking wait.} The pod env sets
\texttt{TORCH\_NCCL\_BLOCKING\_WAIT=1} (\path{scripts/mlsys_pod_env.sh:32}) and the
campaign's remote scripts source it, so every \texttt{wait()} on a collective polls with a
fixed 10\,ms host sleep and a ring whose every round ends in a wait pays one poll period per
round, which \S\ref{sec:res-decode} measures. Removing the variable is not a drop-in,
because it exposes an ordering hazard in the ring's buffer reuse
(\S\ref{app:diagnosis}); the numbers here describe the engine as it ran.
% SRC: results/lcfm/BLOCKING_WAIT_DIAG.md; results/mlsys/bw_diag_p1,p2; scripts/mlsys_pod_env.sh

\paragraph{Draft conditioning.} The draft sees the leading BOS plus the most recent 4095
tokens (\path{src/models/rasd_inference.py:878}, applied and logged at every draft prefill in
the rows reported here).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What a greedy acceptance number is measuring}
\label{sec:res-degeneracy}

The campaign behind this paper began under greedy decoding, and its acceptance numbers were
high in a way that deserved suspicion: a draft that proposes the token the target would emit
anyway is accepted, and a target that has fallen into a loop is maximally predictable. We
measured the degeneracy instead of assuming it. The detector is the project's absolute
repetition rule: a verbatim repeat span of 128 tokens or more, or $60\%$ of tokens inside a
repeated 16-gram, or a periodic tail covering $50\%$ of the row.

Figure~\ref{fig:degeneracy} is the census over the rows that ship with a per-row verdict,
grouped by the temperature each row \emph{records} rather than by its stage name: the 128\,k
temperature campaign ships two stages, one at $T{=}0$ and one at $T{=}1.0$. Under greedy,
\textbf{9 of 10} rows in the long-context first-window audit loop and \textbf{1 of 5} in the
128\,k greedy arm does; at $T{=}1.0$ none of five does; at $T{=}0.7$ \textbf{1 of 77} does. That row is \texttt{ROPE\_EXT\_SAMP\_F16\_SPEC\_pg19\_train\_2768\_s42},
a rotary arm on book 2768 with 77\% of its tokens inside a repeated 16-gram and a zero
periodic tail, which is why the figure marks it with the detector's verdict instead of the
share. Six greedy rows with eight generated tokens each are excluded: too short for a detector
whose shortest repeat span is 128 tokens. The looping row is inside the factor-16 mean of
\S\ref{sec:res-rope}, which we therefore report with and without it.
% SRC: scripts/lcfm_figures.py census(); results/mlsys/natural_f1_128k_first_window.csv; repetition_SA_TEMP_{0,1_0}_128K_SPEC.csv; n5_extension/repetition_ROPE_EXT_SAMP_F16_SPEC.csv

Three consequences are applied here rather than merely noted. The headline protocol is
sampled at $T{=}0.7$, with the earlier greedy numbers reported separately. A looping row is
named wherever one appears, never smoothed into a mean. And the temperature ablation points
\emph{up}: at 128\,k, $T{=}1.0$ reaches acceptance 0.7369 against 0.4681 at the headline
$T{=}0.7$, with 0 of 5 rows degenerate in both arms.

\subsection{Rotary stretching: acceptance and perplexity dissociate}
\label{sec:res-rope}

The natural assumption is that an intervention which costs little in perplexity will cost
little in acceptance, because the draft and the target see the same positions. We tested it
on \texttt{meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B} at 128\,k, with five books per arm and the arms differing
in nothing but \texttt{rope\_factor}.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{figB_rope.pdf}
\caption{Left: speculative acceptance per arm, with the five books drawn as points so the
direction can be checked per book rather than only in the mean. Right: the paired perplexity
ratio against the native arm, from a single passage. Stretching the rotary basis costs
$0.05\%$ and $1.56\%$ of perplexity on that passage and $27\%$ and $31\%$ of acceptance over
five books.}
\label{fig:rope}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:rope} is the measurement, and Table~\ref{tab:rope} the table. Acceptance falls from
\textbf{0.7272} (native) to \textbf{0.5336} (factor 16) and \textbf{0.5006} (factor 32), a \textbf{27} to \textbf{31\%} relative loss, while the paired
perplexity ratio moves by \textbf{$+0.05\%$} and \textbf{$+1.56\%$}. The direction holds on
five books of five, so it is not a book effect, and because the arms differ in nothing else
the drop is a \textbf{rotary effect across three arms}. One row needs naming: the factor-16
mean of $0.5336$ contains \texttt{ROPE\_EXT\_SAMP\_F16\_SPEC\_pg19\_train\_2768\_s42}, the
looping row of \S\ref{sec:res-degeneracy}, whose acceptance of $0.7121$ is the highest in its
arm. Excluding it the factor-16 mean is \textbf{$0.4890$} over the remaining four books, a
$32.8\%$ loss, so the exclusion makes the effect larger, not smaller, and the claim uses the
five-book mean and reports both.

A speculative decoder is only as good as the agreement between the draft's proposal
distribution and the target's. Position scaling is normally evaluated by perplexity, and the
one passage this campaign scored says it is nearly free here. The same intervention costs 27
to 31\% of the acceptance over five books, and on this model the perplexity check did not
reveal it, so acceptance under any change to the target's positional treatment has to be
measured. The claim rests on the five-book acceptance contrast; the perplexity side is one
passage and is reported as such rather than generalised. Every arm here ran on the old engine,
so its absolute levels sit on the pre-fix decode path, and both arms paid the same
blocking-wait cost.

\subsection{Coherence at 1\,M on a natively long-context target}
\label{sec:res-coherence}

The perplexity comparison above cannot reach 1\,M, so we check the long-context property
directly on a target trained to 1\,M
(\texttt{gradientai/Llama-3-8B-Instruct-Gradient-1048k}), where nothing is being stretched and
the question is only whether the model is still a model. The rungs are matched by
\texttt{continuation\_sha256} and the recorded window offsets, never by a pool name.

On the \textbf{one exactly aligned book} the target's perplexity is \textbf{1.805 at 1\,M}
against \textbf{1.649 at 128\,k}, a \textbf{$+9.5\%$} move. That book is
\texttt{pg19\_train\_0}, the King James Bible in the PG-19 pool, and its perplexity of 1.65
means the text is close to deterministic for this model, so it is the strongest and also the
most memorised of the three. On the two books that share 91 to 95\% of their scored tokens the
difference is within \textbf{1.1\%} at perplexities of 10.6 and 12.8. A natively long-context target therefore stays coherent across an eightfold context increase,
which is the property the campaign depends on, though the strongest form of that claim rests on
one near-deterministic book. The measurement is prefill-only, so the decode fix cannot move it.
% SRC: scripts/lcfm_figures.py; results/mlsys/sampling_session/session_a_QUALITY_FIX_{128K,1M}.csv; alignment.csv

\subsection{The ring's key/value rotation was the decode step}
\label{sec:res-decode}

At 128\,k, each decode token requires 7 rotations over the 32 patched layers
(\path{src/models/ring_attention_kernel.py:402} loops over the ranks and line 426 issues a
rotation only while \texttt{step < world\_size - 1}), so \textbf{224 rounds} of a
\texttt{batch\_isend\_irecv} ring per token, measured under the NCCL blocking wait of
\S\ref{sec:setup}, in which PyTorch's \texttt{WorkNCCL::wait} polls with a fixed 10\,ms host
sleep (\texttt{kSynchronizeBusyWaitMillis}, \texttt{ProcessGroupNCCL.cpp:426} in the pinned
torch 2.5.1). Figure~\ref{fig:ringbench} measures that primitive with no model attached, over
five payloads from 4\,KB to 266\,MB, in both wait modes in one session on one box.

Under the blocking wait every payload costs the same ${\sim}10.3$\,ms: 10.3347\,ms for
4\,KB against 10.3520\,ms for 266\,MB, an \textbf{0.4\% spread} across a
66{,}584$\times$ payload range. That flat line is the poll, not the wire. Without the flag the
same primitive takes \textbf{0.134\,ms} at 4\,KB and rises with payload to
\textbf{2.619\,ms} at 266\,MB, which is \textbf{101.7\,GB/s}, and the two modes' post-sync
times agree to within 3\% at every payload, so the wire never changed.
% SRC: scripts/lcfm_figures.py bench_two_modes(), removed_time(); results/mlsys/bw_diag_p1/bench/ringbench_{blocking,async}.csv; results/mlsys/decode_df/ring_bench/ringbench.csv

Removing the rotation is prior work (\S\ref{sec:related}), adopted here as \textbf{split-KV}:
each rank computes attention for the replicated query against only its own prefill shard and
the partials are merged by one MAX and one SUM all-reduce. Our contribution is the diagnosis
and the measurement, not the technique. Split-KV's own times are blocking-wait times too, since
its per-layer merges are synchronous collectives and pay their own share of the same poll.

\subsection{Speculation still pays, and the baseline is corrected}
\label{sec:res-spec}
\label{sec:res-seeds}

With the rotation gone, the question the paper started from can be asked on an engine whose
decode step is no longer dominated by a collective. The answer is yes, on
\textbf{matched books only}, which matters at 128\,k where the target-only arm is a single
book:
\textbf{1.54$\times$ / 2.29$\times$ / 2.20$\times$} decode-only at 128\,k / 512\,k / 1\,M over
the same engine's own target-only path, over 1, 3 and 5 matched books. The 128\,k figure is
the one that changed: the older 2.01$\times$ divided a three-book spec mean by a one-book
target mean, and on the one book both sides measured it is 1.54$\times$. The per-book values are in Appendix~\ref{app:baseline}. End-to-end, where prefill is included, the same comparison reads
$1.50\times$ / $1.76\times$ / $1.41\times$, because at 1\,M a row is 520 to 740\,s depending
on the arm, of which about 340\,s is prefill: with the decode step fixed, prefill is the long
pole and any statement about 1\,M throughput has to say which of the two it means.
% SRC: scripts/lcfm_figures.py f5_rows(); results/mlsys/rerun_r1,r2 + rerun_topup (matched books)


Two of those denominators used to be one and two books. We topped them up after the
confirmatory run, because a ratio whose denominator is one row is a ratio of one row to
another. The 512\,k target-only mean is $2.148$\,s/token over three books against the $2.346$
single-book value it replaces, and the 1\,M mean is $3.155$ over five against $3.211$ over
two. Both moves are downward, so the quoted ratios are lower than the thin ones: the thin
denominator was flattering, by $8\%$ at 512\,k. The book-to-book spread inside the fixed
engine is 1.27$\times$ at 512\,k and 1.30$\times$ at 1\,M, wider than the 1.02$\times$ spread
across seeds, so books and seeds are not interchangeable units.

\paragraph{The corrected old-engine baseline.} Two stages of the seed ablation, the ones meant
to run seeds 123 and 456 at 1\,M, ran seed 42 instead, because the config builder takes the
seed list from the defaults and then overwrites whatever a level declared:
\path{run_experiment.py:693} pops \texttt{seeds} from \texttt{defaults}, line 713 merges the

\paragraph{The speedups are not bought with a different model.} An exactly equivalent kernel
can still move an acceptance number, because the accept/reject decision compares the target's
distribution against the draft's proposal, so one flipped near-tie changes the emitted prefix
and every later proposal with it. We checked the kernel directly: the target-only stream under

diverges at a step where the baseline's own top-2 logit margin is \textbf{0.0000}, against a
row median of 2.9375, with prefill perplexity identical to six decimals.

\subsection{Comparison against a production engine}
\label{sec:res-vllm}

We benchmark vLLM 0.6.3 at tensor-parallel 8 on the same prompts, fed the same token ids from
our own sidecars, so the units match. The comparison is \textbf{not precision-matched}: vLLM
runs \textbf{bf16 weights with a bf16 KV cache and greedy decoding}, while RASD runs
\textbf{FP4 weights with an NF4 KV cache} at $T{=}0.7$ with speculation, and RASD's times are
the split-KV engine measured \textbf{under NCLL blocking wait}. vLLM ran at its default
chunked-prefill and batch-token settings, untuned. Every ratio below carries all three
caveats.

Table~\ref{tab:vllm} in Appendix~\ref{app:baseline} carries the numbers; two readings point in
opposite directions.
\textbf{vLLM's entire run takes 2.04$\times$ to 2.57$\times$ our prefill}, meaning its
prefill plus its 128 decode steps against our first-token time; prefill against prefill the
ratio is 2.52$\times$ at 512\,k and 2.56$\times$ at 1\,M. We do not quote a 128\,k prefill
ratio, because our own 128\,k first-token time varied by about 40\% between sessions with an
identical configuration (8.7 to 12.4\,s), as large as the ratio itself; that prefill advantage
is a property of the ring prefill and is untouched by the decode fix. And \textbf{vLLM's
decode is 130 to 162$\times$ faster per token than ours even after the fix}, against 429 to
731$\times$ before it: the fix removed 70 to 81\% of the decode deficit and left our decode
far behind a production engine, and speculation does not close the gap (our spec decode is
$65\times$, $65\times$ and $59\times$ vLLM's).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Sample size.} The matched comparison is 3 spec and 1 target-only cell at
        128\,k, 3 and 1 at 512\,k, 13 and 2 at 1\,M, and the 128\,k spec-over-target ratio is
        quoted on the one book both sides measured. The five units in a five-book arm are
        books, not seeds, and the book spread is wider than the seed spread.
  \item \textbf{Every timing was taken under NCCL blocking wait} (\S\ref{sec:setup}), which
        inflates the decode times of both engines on this path and of split-KV's own
        synchronous merges; split-KV was not measured without it. The flag is also not a free
        win, because removing it makes the engine emit a different token stream in one
        speculation cell and fail a device-side assertion in three of five diagnostic stages.
        This work reports that hazard and does not fix it (Appendix~\ref{app:diagnosis}).
  % TODO AMAN: Fig 4 (decode against context) and the RoPE and vLLM tables moved to the
% appendices to hold the main text at 8 pages, as Part 3 asked. Say if you want any of
% them back in the main text and I will cut prose instead.
\item \textbf{One node, one fabric, one precision.} Everything is 8$\times$A100 80\,GB SXM4
        over NVLink, there is no bf16 row, and the per-round quantity \S\ref{sec:res-decode}
        measures is a host-side poll at this configuration, so it is not extrapolated to a
        multi-node fabric.
  \item \textbf{Acceptance under split-KV is explained, not tested.} The 1\,M acceptance is
        unchanged over 13 matched cells while per seed it moves both ways inside a 23.0\%
        old-engine spread; the mechanism we offer, one flipped near-tie changing the emitted
        prefix, was not tested directly.
  \item \textbf{Prefill is now the 1\,M pole} and the perplexity side of F1 is one passage. A
        1\,M row is 520 to 740\,s depending on the arm, about 340\,s of it prefill, so the
        end-to-end 1\,M speedup is $1.41\times$ against $2.20\times$ decode-only; and the
        perplexity ratio was scored on a different prompt from the acceptance rows at a native
        perplexity of 1.013, so the F1 claim rests on the acceptance contrast over five books.
  \item \textbf{An unattributed elementwise term.} In the profiled 128\,k decode window,
        19.3\% of device time was elementwise kernels never attributed to an operation; with
        the rotation gone that is a larger share of what remains.
  \item \textbf{Two withdrawn claims are recorded rather than deleted} (Appendix
        \ref{app:changes}): the ``communication is at most $1.2\%$ of wall time'' claim, whose
        bucket recorded 0.31 to 0.37$\times$ of the physical floor, and the $4.4\times$
        ``native context'' speedup from one pair in one session.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked what limits speculative decoding at a million tokens, and the answer arrived in
three parts, none of them about speculation itself. Under greedy decoding the acceptance
numbers this kind of campaign reports are measured on degenerate text: 9 of 10 long-context
rows in the released first-window audit loop, and 1 of 5 rows in the 128\,k greedy arm, against
1 of 77 sampled rows. Once sampled at $T{=}0.7$, stretching a model's rotary basis costs 27 to
31\% of the acceptance over five books for $0.05$ to $1.56\%$ of perplexity on one passage,
so the perplexity check did not reveal the loss. A natively 1M-trained target stays coherent at
1\,M, within $9.5\%$ of its own 128\,k perplexity on the exactly aligned book.

What was hostile was our engine, and the first thing we learned about it is that our diagnosis
was the measurement condition. Rotating each layer's key/value shard around eight ranks costs
224 rounds per decode token, and each round ended in a host-side wait that, under the NCCL
blocking wait every run here used, polls with a fixed 10\,ms sleep: the same
${\sim}10.3$\,ms per round at 4\,KB and at 266\,MB, and 0.134\,ms rising to 2.619\,ms once
the flag is removed. Adopting decode-side context parallelism, which is prior work, is worth
$2.80$ to $4.38\times$ decode-only with speculation and $3.30$ to $5.32\times$ without it,
and the time it removes is the poll period at 128\,k and the serialised transfer at 512\,k
and 1\,M, growing from 2.4 to 13.9\,s per token because the shard a round ships grows with
the context. Speculation still pays $1.54$ to $2.29\times$ on top on matched books, the
matched 1\,M acceptance is unchanged at $-0.1\%$, and against vLLM at bf16 and greedy
decoding, not a precision-matched comparison, vLLM's entire run takes $2.04$ to $2.57\times$
our prefill while its decode stays $130$ to $162\times$ faster per token: the rotation was the
decode pathology, and the flag that made it look flat is a configuration detail we can name.

\paragraph{Correctness.} The target-only stream under split-KV is bit-identical to the ring
baseline for twelve consecutive tokens and then diverges at a step where the baseline's own
top-2 logit margin is \textbf{0.0000}, against a row median of 2.9375 and a minimum of 0.0000:
an exact tie broken the other way. Greedy repeats the pattern, and the speculative row
diverges at index 1 with margin 0.8750 against a row median of 0.9375, below the row's own
median. Prefill perplexity is identical to six decimals under both configurations, and the
perplexity gate is in any case blind to a decode-path change, because it scores a prefill
forward with \texttt{use\_cache=False}.

\section{Ablations kept from the reviewed version}
\label{app:ablations}

The design-point sweeps are \textbf{old-engine} results on the \textbf{synthetic repeated
paragraph} at 64\,k on 8$\times$A100 40\,GB, three seeds on one prompt, so the bracketed
figures are ranges over those three runs and not confidence intervals. They are kept because
they document how the system's knobs were chosen, and because two of the reviewed version's
readings of them were wrong: the chunk size is a transmission chunk and does not bound memory,
and prefetch depth 2 executes the same code path as depth 1, so that axis had two arms and not
three. The block-size effect is specific to the ring decode path and is inert under split-KV.
These runs predate the draft-window fix of \S\ref{sec:setup} and are affected by it
(Appendix~\ref{app:changes}, E6); no claim here rests on their acceptance.

\paragraph{The ceiling test, in one place.} Vanilla HuggingFace \texttt{generate()} with
FlashAttention-2, bf16 weights and a bf16 KV cache, single rank, no quantization and no
sequence parallelism, on \texttt{meta-llama/Llama-2-7b-hf}: 32\,k completes at 30.1\,GiB and
7.25\,tok/s, and every context of 128\,k and above runs out of memory during prefill. The
model differs from the targets used for the throughput results (\S\ref{sec:setup}), so this is
a capability reference for the standard stack and nothing more; no ratio in this paper uses
it.
